MARRY ME
Some questions are asked because we need an answer.

And some questions are asked because, somewhere in our hearts, we've already begun imagining what the answer might feel like.

Will you marry me?

It is only a few words, yet somehow they carry an entire lifetime inside them.

Whenever I hear this song, I don't immediately imagine a wedding.

I imagine the years before it.

The countless ordinary days that would have to exist before two people could ever arrive at that one extraordinary moment.

I imagine growing older with someone.

Not just growing older beside them, but actually knowing them through all the versions they become.

Knowing the person they are now.

Meeting the person they will be five years from now.

Ten years from now.

And perhaps one day, looking at them and realizing that although so much has changed, there is still something familiar in the way they smile.

Maybe that's what forever really is.

Not one long, perfect moment.

But thousands of ordinary moments choosing the same person.

Breakfasts.

Long days.

Quiet nights.

Celebrations.

Arguments that eventually become stories you laugh about.

Apologies.

New beginnings.

The kind of life that isn't always beautiful in the way movies make it seem, but beautiful because you built it together.

I know we're still young.

There are still so many things ahead of us that we haven't experienced. There are places we haven't been, dreams we haven't reached, and lessons we haven't learned.

So I don't want to pretend that I already know exactly what our future looks like.

I don't.

And maybe that's what makes this feeling so sincere.

I don't know what tomorrow holds.

I simply know that when I imagine a future worth looking forward to, sometimes, without meaning to, I see you in it.

I see a version of us that has grown beyond who we are today.

A version that has survived seasons we haven't even entered.

A version that can look back at our younger selves and say,

We made it here.

And perhaps, if that day ever comes, the most beautiful part won't be the question itself.

It will be everything that happened before it.

Every little decision.

Every difficult conversation.

Every ordinary day.

Every time we chose to understand.

Every time we stayed long enough to see another chapter.

Because a lifetime isn't created in one question.

It is created in all the days that come after the answer.

And if someday, somewhere in our future, you ask me that question—

I hope I remember this version of us.

The younger ones who didn't know where life would take them, but still dared to imagine forever.
